# Crowns of the Frontier — Full Game Audit

**Date:** 10 November 2026
**Version audited:** 2.6.1 (`package.json`), 155 commits, working tree at `e5154ab`
**Scope:** every subsystem — simulation, AI, rendering, animation, audio, content, UI/UX, accessibility, performance, networking, build/release, mobile and store readiness
**Method:** full source read, test-suite execution, benchmark artefact analysis, screenshot review, asset measurement, live browser launch

---

## 1. Verdict in one page

**Is it world class? No. Is it a serious, unusually disciplined project? Yes.**

This is not a hobby prototype. It is a **complete, playable, tested, single-player 3D RTS** with 25,665 lines of JavaScript, 749 automated tests, an authoritative multiplayer server, a Blender asset pipeline, a WebGPU/WebGL dual renderer, and a genuinely well art-directed front end. In engineering discipline it sits in the top few percent of solo/indie browser projects. I have reviewed very few independent browser RTS games with this level of verification.

But the question asked is whether it **rivals world-class 3D RTS games on desktop and mobile**. Measured against *Age of Empires II: Definitive Edition*, *Age of Empires IV*, *StarCraft II* or *Company of Heroes 3*, it does not — and the gap is not a matter of polish or more time. It is structural, in four specific places:

| Structural gap | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **No skeletal animation** | `skins:0, animations:0` in every GLB. All motion is procedural joint rotation in code. This alone separates it from every commercial RTS of the last 20 years. |
| **Frame rate below playable** | Its own benchmark: **24.4 FPS at Low, 22.9 at Medium, 11.0 at High** in a crowded 8-player battle against a 60 FPS target (`artifacts/battle-smoothness-performance.json`). |
| **Shallow strategic layer** | 3 civilisations, 11 technologies, 8 recurring strategic decisions. *AoE II DE* has 45 civilisations, ~90 technologies, and dozens of interacting systems. |
| **Content volume** | 10 missions × 3 objectives, **424 words** of campaign dialogue, 29 synthesised audio cues, no music, no voice acting. |

**Overall product score: 3.4 / 10 against commercial RTS standards.**
**Engineering score: 7.2 / 10.**
**"Can it rival world-class 3D RTS on desktop or mobile?" — No, not in its current architecture. It is roughly at the level of a strong 2010-era mobile/indie RTS in presentation, and a competent 2000-era RTS in systems depth, wrapped in 2026-grade tooling.**

## 4. Area-by-area audit

### 4.1 Art, models and rendering — **3.5 / 10**

**What exists.** 126 models generated in Blender, exported through `gltfpack`/Meshopt, validated against a manifest, split into three detail catalogues (`frontier-low` 4.6 MB, `-balanced` 13.7 MB, `-near` 17.7 MB) plus a shared tree atlas. Buildings are merged by material into a handful of draw calls; units are merged per type into a single draw using a per-instance pose texture sampled in the vertex shader. 218 draw calls with a full town is genuinely good engineering.

**What it looks like.** At close zoom the town centre is a stack of flat-shaded boxes with a solid blue prism roof. The knight is a sphere head on a cylinder torso with stick limbs and no facial detail. Trees are stacked spheres with a flat canopy texture. Ground is a smooth green plane with a few grass sprites. There is no ambient occlusion, no bloom, no depth of field, no anti-aliasing beyond MSAA, and no baked global illumination.

**Why.** The models are **parametric geometry generated by Python scripts**, not sculpted art. `scripts/blender/human-figures.py` builds bodies by lathing 10-sided tubes through ring profiles (`section(role, center, rings, motion, sides=10)`). That produces clean, consistent, correctly-proportioned figures — and it will never produce the surface detail, silhouette variation, or material richness of hand-modelled art. No amount of shader work fixes this.

**Texture budget.** All materials are **1024²**, on a 320 m map, plus a 1536² runtime canvas for macro ground variation. Commercial RTS games use 2K–4K tiling materials with virtual texturing. The ground goes visibly soft the moment the camera drops below tactical height.

**Shadowing.** `PCFShadowMap` (not PCFSoft, not VSM), 1024/2048, refreshed at 20–30 Hz. Visible stair-stepping and shadow lag on moving armies.

**Honest note.** The art *direction* is coherent — the half-timbered Alder, sun-bleached Sunward and dark Raven palettes read clearly, and the age-progression material promotion (timber → plaster → stone) is a genuinely clever systemic solution. But direction is not production.

### 4.2 Animation — **2.5 / 10**

This is the single largest gap and it is not closeable by iteration.

- **Zero skeletal animation.** Every GLB reports `skins: 0, animations: 0`.
- Motion is procedural joint rotation: `src/world-polish.js:77-98` handles walk, wheels, horse legs, knees, torso, head, cape, sail, oar, ram, siege arm.
- ~12–14 discrete states: idle, walk, melee swing, bow draw/release, five work strokes, carry, heal, siege crank, sail/oar, death topple.
- **No blend trees, no transition clips, no additive layers, no foot IK.** Feet are placed by a sampler (`src/foot-placement.js`), not solved.
- Death is a rigid topple-and-sink (`src/battle-remains.js:87`).

Commercial RTS units carry 40–120 bones and 30–80 clips with layered upper-body action, additive breathing, directional hit reactions and blended locomotion. That is what makes *AoE IV* units feel alive at 30 pixels tall. Procedural rotation cannot reproduce weight, overlap, anticipation or secondary motion — the exact qualities that separate "a figure that moves" from "a soldier".

### 4.3 Gameplay depth — **3.0 / 10**

Well-built, shallow.

**Economy.** Four resources. Flat gather rates (food 3.1/s, wood 2.9/s, gold/stone 2.6/s), carry capacity 18, real walk-back to drop-off buildings. **Farms are infinite** — pay 65 wood once, harvest forever; no reseed cycle, no depletion. Market is two fixed buttons (100 → 70 gold; 130 gold → 100). No trade routes, no price drift, no relics, no hunting or herding.

**Combat.** Damage is `max(1, attack − max(0, armor − penetration))` — **one armour value per unit**, no melee/pierce split. Counters are a three-way rock-paper-scissors plus siege. **Projectiles always hit** — no accuracy roll, no friendly fire, no collision. Splash only on trebuchet and siege galleon. Elevation gives ±25% but **only on 6 of 14 maps**.

**Formations and stances.** 3 formations, 2 stances. Garrison is **villagers only** — you cannot put archers in a tower, which removes a core defensive decision that *AoE II* players use constantly.

**Technology.** 11 technologies, 22 total levels. Five are pure linear stat ladders capped at `age + 1`. There are **no tech chains, no branching, no mutually exclusive choices**. It is a flat list wearing a tree costume.

**Pathfinding.** Competent A* on a 2-unit grid, 8-neighbour, binary heap, BFS region labelling, line-of-sight fast path, stuck recovery at 1.8 s, 0.2 separation push. Fine to 160 units. No flow field, no formation pathing, no unit pushing.

**Decision count.** A player makes roughly **eight meaningful strategic decisions per match**, several of them near-forced. *AoE II* offers dozens per age, with build orders that differ by civilisation and matchup.

**Verdict.** The *execution* quality is above hobby grade — melee footprint contact, wall link graphs, and route-validated AI fortifications are real engineering. The *decision* depth is roughly **15–25% of AoE II's strategic surface**.

### 4.4 AI — **4.0 / 10**

**Architecture.** One ~140-line if/else priority ladder (`src/commander-ai.js:184-324`) inside a four-state string FSM (`muster / defend / regroup / attack`). Unit production is a greedy utility score; building order is a hardcoded array. Tick cadence 1.4–4.8 s depending on difficulty.

**What it does well — and this deserves credit:**
- **It does not cheat.** Costs are never difficulty-scaled; affordability is checked against real stock; tests assert no free units. Difficulty is behavioural only (20→48 workers, 65→220 s first attack, 1.44 s vs 4.8 s decisions).
- **It is fog-honest.** Sightings are per-owner and expire after 70–160 s.
- **It counter-composes** from remembered sightings (spearmen weight ×2 per observed cavalry, etc.).
- **Real naval logic** — boarding, landing, beach retry.
- **Path-validated wall layouts** and perimeter fortification.

**Where it collapses.** Four exploits kill it in under two minutes:
1. **Scout feint.** Any damaged unit near any asset — including a farm — flips the AI to `defend` and sends the *entire* army to that point. Ping-pong one scout and its army never fights.
2. **Production ceiling.** `if (!buildings.some(b => b.type === type))` means exactly **one** barracks, range, stable and workshop for the whole match, each capped at queue length 2. Four barracks out-produce it permanently.
3. **No mill.** `mill` is absent from the build order, so its farmers always walk to the town centre.
4. **Walls never start** while any threat exists — one unit poking the base every 30 s prevents fortification indefinitely.

It also never rebuilds a lost town centre in skirmish (`if (!tc) return`), and expansion sends a **single** worker up to 110 units away.

**Verdict.** Roughly *AoE II DE* "Moderate" minus the cheating — a credible punching bag, not an opponent. It will not punish greed, will not raid your economy with a dedicated squad, and cannot recover from a decapitation strike.

### 4.5 Content — **3.0 / 10**

| Content | Count | Commercial reference |
|---|---:|---|
| Civilisations | **3** | AoE II DE: 45 |
| Units | **18** | AoE II DE: ~35 lines, 100+ with unique units |
| Buildings | **19** | AoE II DE: ~30 |
| Technologies | **11** (22 levels) | AoE II DE: ~90 |
| Ages | **4** | AoE II: 4 · AoE IV: 4 |
| Maps | **14** | AoE II DE: 100+ |
| Game modes | **9** | Strong for the scale |
| Campaign chapters | **10** | AoE II DE: 40+ campaigns, 200+ scenarios |
| Objectives per chapter | **3** | AoE II: 5–15 with triggers and events |
| Campaign dialogue | **424 words** | AoE II DE: hundreds of thousands |

Campaign scripting is real — objectives are predicate functions over live simulation state, with markers, optional medals and checkpoints (`src/campaigns.js:33-121`). But 424 words of story across ten chapters is a paragraph per mission. There are no scripted set pieces, no reinforcements on triggers, no branching, no cinematic camera.

**This is a well-made small game, not a commercial content volume.** A player finishes everything here in roughly 4–6 hours.

### 4.6 Audio — **3.0 / 10**

- **29 audio cues**, all **synthesised at build time** by `scripts/author-audio-samples.mjs`, dedicated CC0.
- **No music.** One 111 KB ambient `pad.wav`. No composed tracks, no adaptive layers, no combat intensity response.
- **No voice acting.** `src/voice-system.js` synthesises "barks" from vowel formants (F1/F2/F3 filters) — the units make vowel-shaped noises, not words.
- 3D positioning, distance attenuation and a procedural Web Audio fallback are implemented, and the fallback is a genuinely good resilience decision.

*AoE II DE* ships a full orchestral score and **thousands** of recorded acknowledgement lines per civilisation. Audio is half of game feel; here it is a placeholder.

### 4.7 UI / UX — **6.5 / 10**

**The landing screen is the best thing in the project.** Restrained editorial layout, serif display type, dark green palette, clear hierarchy, seven mode cards, live edition label. It would not look out of place on a commercial indie store page. The only flaw: the 3D backdrop behind it is essentially black — a world-class RTS menu shows a living vista.

**In-game HUD** is genuinely complete: resource bar, age, timer, speed control, 200×200 minimap, selection panel with portrait/HP/attack/armour/range/vision, command card, production queue with per-item cancel, objective panel with per-mode progress, toasts, order summaries, blocked-order feedback. Almost nothing is a stub.

**Phone layout** is thoughtful, not an afterthought: a compact HUD activates on coarse pointers, collapses objectives, shrinks the minimap, and enforces 44 px touch targets.

**What fails:**
- `user-scalable=no` in the viewport — a **WCAG 1.4.4 failure** that blocks zoom entirely.
- **No colourblind mode**, despite team colours carrying all unit identification.
- Attack-move and stop — two of the most-used commands in any RTS — are hidden behind a "More" button on phone.
- No camera-location hotkeys, no select-all-building keys, no queue-waypoint preview, no rally-point editing, no minimap ping.
- Modal opens focus on the close button and leaves the background tabbable.
- 8–9 px labels on phone.
- **Onboarding is a checklist overlay on a normal skirmish** — no scripted camera, no gated steps, no coach marks. Help is a wall of prose.

### 4.8 Performance — **2.0 / 10**

The project's own benchmark, in `artifacts/battle-smoothness-performance.json`, on a crowded 8-player 400×400 battle at 160 population:

| Quality | Measured FPS | Target | Simulation speed |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Low | **24.4** | 60 | 0.99× |
| Medium | **22.9** | 60 | 1.00× |
| High | **11.0** | 60 | 0.81× |

At High the simulation itself falls to **81% real time** — the game runs in slow motion. This is the development machine, and it is the single most disqualifying number in the audit. `QUALITY_TARGETS.md` correctly states these are acceptance targets not achievements — but the target is missed by 2.5–5.5×.

Contributing factors: 851k triangles of authored geometry with **no geometric LOD for units or buildings** (trees are the only LOD system), 70 MB of assets, per-frame fog texture updates, and a renderer with no occlusion culling beyond frustum tests.

**Load time.** 70 MB over a 20 Mbps connection is ~28 s before first play, and the service worker precaches the entire catalogue on install.

### 4.9 Multiplayer — **5.5 / 10**

**Architecture is correct.** Authoritative server running the real `Simulation`, 10 Hz tick, 5 Hz snapshot, WebSocket JSON with permessage-deflate, command validation against ownership and fog, per-player perspective filtering. Reconnect with a 120 s grace period and token resume, tested across seven lifecycle cases. Chat is XSS-safe (`textContent`), rate-limited, control-character stripped.

**It cannot scale.** A measured 8-player snapshot is **182,773 bytes**, sent 5× per second — **~0.91 MB/s (~7.3 Mbps) per player**, ~58 Mbps of egress for one room. Production runs `MAX_ROOMS=1` on a free Render instance with in-memory rooms that vanish on restart.

**Missing entirely:** matchmaking, lobby browser, accounts, ranked/ELO, anti-cheat beyond ownership checks, replays, live spectating, regional servers, persistence. Pause-on-any-disconnect freezes seven players for two minutes when one rage-quits.

**Tier: polished private-room co-op.** Not a public multiplayer service.

### 4.10 Engineering quality — **7.2 / 10**

**Genuinely excellent:**
- 749 tests including real HTTP/WebSocket servers, GLB parsing, geometry assertions and frame-time soaks.
- Documentation that is **honest about its own gaps** — `QUALITY_TARGETS.md` explicitly says "The current beta has not reached that goal." That is rare and valuable.
- Release manifest with build ID, commit stamping and asset-hash verification; `/health` returns 503 on a stale package rather than a false OK.
- Two runtime dependencies.
- Consistent, readable naming; small focused modules (median file ~60 lines).

**Genuinely poor:**
- **Code is written as dense one-liners.** `src/main.js` averages **205 characters per line** with 100 lines over 300 characters; it packs 116 KB into 561 lines. `simulation.js` averages 118. This is not a style preference — it makes review, diffing, debugging and onboarding substantially harder, and it is the main reason a second developer could not join productively.
- **No linter, no formatter, no TypeScript, no type checking.** No ESLint, Prettier, tsconfig or editorconfig anywhere in the repo.
- CI runs only `npm test` and `npm run build` — no lint, no bundle-size budget, no visual regression, no cross-browser matrix.
- **3 tests currently failing** and committed red: `building:keep:alder:0` and `building:workshop:alder:0` "have daylight beneath the original footprint" — i.e. **buildings visibly float above sloped terrain** at three detail tiers. This is a live visual bug the suite correctly catches and nobody fixed.

### 4.11 Mobile & store readiness — **5.0 / 10** (PWA) / **1.0 / 10** (store)

**What works:** installable PWA, landscape lock, service-worker offline play, safe-area insets (bottom only), 44 px touch targets, pinch-zoom camera, drag pan, double-tap select-same-type, compact HUD, device-memory-based quality defaults.

**What blocks a store release:**
- **No native wrapper** — no Capacitor, Cordova, or equivalent. It cannot be uploaded to Play Console or App Store Connect as it stands.
- **No account system, no cloud save** — progress is `localStorage` only, so it is lost on cache clear or device change.
- **No store assets** — no screenshots, feature graphic, preview video, age rating, privacy policy, or data-safety declaration.
- **No monetisation, no analytics, no crash reporting.**
- **Manifest is minimal** — no `id`, `screenshots`, `categories`, `shortcuts`.
- **No `beforeinstallprompt` handler** — no install UI at all.
- **No i18n.** English only, hardcoded strings.

Even a perfect wrapper would not fix the bigger problem: **the game runs at 11–30 FPS on a development laptop.** On a mid-range Android phone it would be a slideshow. Store certification on mobile is not the blocker — the frame budget is.

## 6. The five things that actually matter

If only five things were fixed, these are the five — in order of impact per unit of work:

1. **Fix the frame rate.** 24 FPS at Low is fatal and no other improvement matters if the game stutters. Add geometric LOD for units and buildings, cut the near-catalogue triangle budget, move fog and shadow updates off the critical path, and cut the 70 MB payload. Target: 60 FPS at Medium on integrated graphics, 30 FPS on a mid-range phone.
2. **Adopt skeletal animation.** Author 6–8 skinned characters in Blender (villager, spearman, archer, knight, siege crew, healer, sovereign) with 12–15 clips each. Retarget the existing procedural rig to clips. This single change does more for perceived quality than any shader work.
3. **Deepen the simulation.** Split melee/pierce armour. Make farms deplete. Add tech prerequisites and two mutually exclusive branch choices per age. Allow archers to garrison towers. Add one more counter relationship. Target: 25 meaningful decisions, not 8.
4. **Hire or license audio.** One composer for 20 minutes of adaptive music; one voice actor for ~120 acknowledgement and order lines per faction. Audio is 50% of perceived production value and it currently does not exist.
5. **Fix the AI's four exploits.** Filter scouts out of threat detection, allow multiple production buildings, add the mill to the build order, and decouple wall building from transient threats. This is a day of work and turns the AI from exploitable to credible.

Then, and only then: store packaging, accounts, matchmaking, localisation.

## 7. What is genuinely good — do not lose it

It would be dishonest to end on the gaps. These are real assets:

- **The test discipline.** 749 tests, real servers, real GLB parsing, frame-time soaks, and tests that fail for the *right* reasons. This is better verification than most commercial indie releases.
- **The honesty of the documentation.** `QUALITY_TARGETS.md`, `RELEASE_READINESS.md` and `ROADMAP.md` state plainly what is not done. That is the single strongest signal that this project can improve — most projects at this stage lie to themselves.
- **The landing screen.** Genuinely commercial-grade art direction. It proves the visual judgement is there; the 3D execution has not caught up.
- **The renderer engineering.** 218 draw calls with a full town, per-instance pose textures, WebGPU/WebGL parity, a release manifest with hash verification, and a service worker that degrades gracefully. This is senior-level work.
- **The netcode design.** Authoritative, fog-honest, ownership-validated, reconnect-safe. Wrong scale, right architecture.
- **Dependency restraint.** Two runtime packages. No framework. That is discipline.

## 8. Bottom line

**The question was: "is it fully world class, fully professional and standard in all areas — can it rival world-class 3D RTS on desktop and mobile?"**

**No — and it is not close, in four specific and structural ways:** no skeletal animation, frame rate below playable, a simulation roughly a fifth as deep as *AoE II*, and content/audio volume at demo scale.

**But the framing matters.** This is not a failed attempt at a commercial RTS. It is a **complete, tested, honest, genuinely playable browser RTS** built by one person with unusual engineering discipline — and it is roughly 70% of the way through the *engineering* work and 25% of the way through the *production* work. The engineering is done well enough that the production work is now the bottleneck, which is the good problem to have.

Reaching "rivals commercial RTS" is not a matter of more commits. It requires **skinned art, a composer, a voice actor, a performance engineer, and 3–5× the content** — realistically 12–18 months of a small team, or a deliberate decision to compete in the premium-indie mobile niche instead, where the bar is lower and the current build is already credible.

**What it is today, stated plainly: an impressive and unusually rigorous indie RTS that is not yet a commercial product, and whose remaining gap is production, not engineering.**
