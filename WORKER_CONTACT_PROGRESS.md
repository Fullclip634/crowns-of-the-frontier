# Worker tools, resource supply and HUD progress

The installed Blender LTS pipeline authored a solid axe, pick, hammer and sickle for the five villager variants. `node scripts/blender/build-worker-tools.mjs` writes an editable `.blend` and staged GLBs under `.tools/art-candidate/worker-tools/`. The tool patch retains the existing bodies, clothing, rigs, other 121 catalogue roots, and shared tree artwork. The current Low/Near catalogues contain the new tools; Balanced was recomposed from the current sources with `node scripts/blender/build-balanced.mjs --directory <staged catalogue>`. Its 126 models, exact visible triangle positions, UVs and colors, and measured normal error pass the precision checks. The shared tree GLB remains byte identical. Low stays within its 4.125 MiB budget.

Woodcutting uses a two-handed pose: the right hand retains the axe grip, the left reaches the shaft, and the cutting plane follows the forward stroke. The actual Low/Balanced/Near rigs were checked at 101 phases per cycle for grip contact, finite joints and smooth transitions. Villagers navigate to the closer wood range through the normal path and collision rules. An extra final path segment is accepted only when its segment is clear. Fractional grid offsets (128 open approaches), water, hostile walls, saved routes, AI assignment and network work observation have focused regressions. Mining now also uses the two-handed solver and closer approach described below. Construction uses the checked work faces below. Berry picking and crop harvesting now have separate motions and closer approaches, also described below.

Newly generated trees, berries, stone, gold and fish hold twice the previous natural supply, including custom battles. Harvest rates and explicitly placed resource amounts are unchanged. Save and restore retain the exact depleted amount. Gold deposits use a warm ore body and separate metallic inclusions; stone deposits keep their rock material. The top resource bar now shows distinct original SVG icons before each amount and visible FOOD, WOOD, GOLD, STONE and POP labels on desktop and phone layouts, with full accessible quantities.

Custom battles now include an Alliance resources panel. It shows food, wood, gold and stone for each teammate, keeps enemy treasuries hidden in multiplayer snapshots, and lets the player send a chosen integer amount. The server checks team membership, living players, resource type, available balance and the transfer limit before changing either treasury. The recipient and sender receive a gift notice; balances update from the authoritative match state. Solo AI allies use the same transfer rules.

Verification: the full Node regression suite and production build passed after recomposing Balanced. Built gameplay was inspected in WebGPU High at 1024×600, WebGPU Low at 568×320, and WebGL Medium at 740×360. All three displayed golden ore, separate stone, five visible resource labels and icons, and no horizontal overflow; screenshots and measurements are in `artifacts/resource-presentation-browser/`. These browser checks are functional evidence, not performance measurements on a physical phone. The broad RTS quality goal remains active; mining contact, match length/balance, device performance, and multiplayer match play need further work and external player/device validation.

## AI construction route safety

The 16 October Highlands/Vast reproduction showed an AI mine foundation covering a spearman and cutting off his move order. `commanderBuild` now evaluates its proposed solid buildings against nearby allied combat routes using a temporary navigation grid. It skips a site that covers a troop or makes a previously reachable order unreachable, and accepts the site when pathfinding finds a detour. The check leaves the live simulation revision, current orders and cached routes unchanged. A saved-state replay of the exact mine location confirms the old site is still geometrically buildable, the new guard rejects it, and the AI selects another valid site. Four focused regressions and the full 466-test suite passed; production build passed. This addresses this construction failure mode, while crowding, transport routing and performance still need broader match-scale evaluation.

## Custom multiplayer update payload

Recurring custom-battle updates now omit static match setup and simulation fields that clients already received in the initial snapshot, while retaining live entities, players, visibility, resource changes, combat visuals, and the existing privacy sentinels. Initial and reconnect snapshots remain complete. In the eight-human Huge Highlands network fixture, serialized JSON for one live update measured 182,359 bytes versus 451,786 bytes for the full reconnect snapshot (about 60% smaller before WebSocket compression). Network tests verify ally privacy, reconnect restoration, and projectile presentation. All 466 tests and the production build passed. The build still reports oversized JavaScript chunks; this payload change does not address bundle download size or device frame rate.

## Mining approach and two-handed pick

Gold and stone miners now use both hands on the pick handle. The wrist turns the pick points into the forward swing plane instead of sweeping them sideways. Tests load the released Blender meshes in all three detail tiers and sample 1,537 mining phases: both grips remain attached, the support hand stays within one centimeter of the handle, and its motion remains continuous. The ordinary per-unit pose cache also applies to mining; no meshes, textures or draw batches are added.

Profiling the first version showed a repeatable cost from solving arm chains during every steady mining frame. The module now prepares a 513-sample rotation curve before gameplay, occupying about 36 KiB of CPU memory. Normalized quaternion interpolation retains continuous movement across Euler wraps; partial-strength starting and stopping still use the analytic solver. A comparison inside every curve interval checks the sampled transforms against that solver to within 0.001 world units. The final optimized production review is under `artifacts/mining-optimized/`; the earlier unoptimized captures and profiler reports remain separate.

The standard villager gathers ore within 2.8 world units rather than 3.45, approaching to 2.65 rather than 3.1. The pathfinder finishes the final fraction of a grid cell only across a clear segment, using the existing terrain, resource, wall and gate collision grid. The same ranges govern player workers, AI assignment, work animation and network observation. A 1,024-case regression covers both resources, fractional grid offsets, eight starting bearings and nearly exhausted deposits. Other regressions cover water and continuous hostile walls, saved miners delivering the last cargo and selecting another deposit, and observers receiving the same visible work state without private commands. Ten-miner human and AI crews also share deposits for a simulated minute, make repeated deliveries, and conserve all harvested resources without blocked orders. Resource quantities, gather rates and carry capacity are unchanged; shorter walking distances can affect delivery throughput and still need match-balance evaluation.

Ore depletion now lowers the exposed rock height gradually to 85% while retaining its horizontal bedrock footprint. Uniform shrinking to 48% previously left the visible deposit far inside its unchanged collision radius. The footprint check verifies grounded geometry and unchanged quantities through full, half and almost empty states. Full regression: 548 tests pass; production build passes. Production-render reviews cover native High/Balanced/Low, compatibility High, and a 568 by 320 emulated phone, including working-unit picking. Screenshots are under `artifacts/mining-before/`, `artifacts/mining-after/` and `artifacts/mining-optimized/`.

The controlled alternating comparison retains 96 visible miners, one troop mesh group, the same texture counts and the same pose-prefix counts. Desktop WebGL completion medians were 7.2/7.5 ms for the old release and 8.9/8.0 ms for the optimized new animation; phone-sized WebGL medians were 4.1/5.0 ms and 5.5/5.3 ms respectively. The first unoptimized implementation was more expensive, so the curve reduces added cost without eliminating it. The measurements in `artifacts/mining-render-optimized/` serialize GPU completion in a desktop browser: they are not real-game FPS or physical-phone performance claims.

This reduces the mining gap rather than proving exact surface contact from every position. Irregular rock faces and differences in ground height still require contact-target calibration. Food contact, physical-device performance, full match balance and human usability remain open requirements of the larger game goal.

## Building construction and repair contact

Builders now choose reachable positions beside a construction frame's faces and face the work surface. The normal stand-off is 1.08 world units; the accepted contact band leaves room for the worker body, hammer width and final movement tolerance. The released Blender hammer meshes in Low, Balanced and Near cross that work plane at the impact phase and clear it while raised. Building and repair eligibility, animation observation and authoritative network presentation share this contact rule. Mechanical and naval unit repairs retain their separate existing range.

Workers walk out of a newly placed foundation before building. Routes use the ordinary navigation grid until the checked final approach. When a coarse cell is blocked only by the target's radius, the last step uses a private grid retaining water, natural obstacles, other buildings and connected walls. A continuous footprint check rejects crossing the building or clipping its padded corners. The global army grid is unchanged. Crews share cached grids by site, team and navigation revision; per-worker approach state retains only small destination data and is recomputed after restoring a save.

AI wall-clearing move orders now reach their safe destination precisely. The ordinary loose movement tolerance could otherwise leave a builder inside a later foundation after it finished an earlier section. Regressions retain the existing paid-wall and developed-perimeter completion deadlines, allied gates, worker replacement and saved construction plans. Human queues, repair spending, foundation escape, aligned/fractional sites, rotated walls and gates, hostile walls, unbridged water and shoreline dock building also have coverage.

Production before/after images in `artifacts/construction-contact-before/` and `artifacts/construction-contact-after/` use the same house, camera, actual walking simulation and fixed cosmetic stroke phase. They cover High WebGPU at 1000 by 700 and Low WebGL at 568 by 320. The worker's centre moved from about 5.21 to 3.38 world units from the house centre; the new worker faces the facade directly. Both construction and repair render without a graphics error. No character meshes, textures or draw batches are added. These are functional and visual checks, not physical-phone performance certification.

The complete regression suite passes 564 tests. Focused construction, AI fortification and worker-route checks also pass after the final path-transition refinement. A separate alternating CPU diagnostic places 24 real builders at four sites on an Epic map: all sites finish without blocked orders in both versions, and measured setup and steady-work costs remain comparable. That diagnostic excludes rendering and cannot establish device FPS.

This establishes contact with the standard work frame and improves the visible facade gap. Early clipped construction stages, recessed or irregular architecture, and terrain-height differences still need authored contact targets before claiming exact material contact in every situation. Construction cost and progress rates remain unchanged; changed travel and clearing distances need continuing full-match balance review.

## Berry picking and crop harvesting

Food workers now use two motions on the existing Blender villager and sickle model. Berry pickers bend and reach with their free hand while holding the sickle down beside them. Farmers bend toward the crop and sweep the sickle across its head height. The released Low, Balanced and Near meshes are sampled through complete loops to check continuous poses, attached tool grips, picking height and cutting height. These checks establish the intended reach band, not exact hand contact with every berry or crop stalk.

The standard villager's berry work range is 1.9 world units, with a 1.75-unit path approach. Farm range is 3.3, with a 3.15-unit approach. The original forgiving selection footprints remain intact. Human and AI assignment, work eligibility, network observation and ordinary pathfinding share these ranges. Final fractions of a coarse path cell are accepted only across a clear segment; hostile walls and unbridged water stay blocking. Older saved routes refresh their previous approach distance. Resource supply, gather rates and carry capacity are unchanged; shorter delivery journeys still need full-match economic balance evaluation.

The network sends only a cosmetic forage/harvest subtype while a visible worker is actually working. It retains command and route privacy. Local and observed poses use the same work clock; absent subtype data falls back to harvesting. Regression checks cover 256 fractional-cell approaches, human and AI crews making repeated deliveries, exact berry conservation, saved routes, AI assignment and work-state privacy.

Production before/after images in `artifacts/food-contact-before/` and `artifacts/food-contact-after/` use the same camera, real gathering simulation and fixed cosmetic phases. High WebGPU at 1000 by 700 and Low WebGL at 568 by 320 both pass without a graphics error. Corresponding draw-call readings match. No additional models, meshes, textures or draw groups are introduced. These controlled views do not establish device frame rates, fully natural human motion, or precise contact on every slope and resource variant.

The complete local suite passed 569 tests and the production build passed. The final 11 food and unit-action tests also passed after adding explicit AI-assignment, older-save and farm-construction observer coverage. A subsequent production review outside the level starting pad passes both renderers in `artifacts/food-contact-slope/`; the berry worker and resource bases differ in elevation by about 0.26 world units. These images still do not cover every slope. Generated review images, measurement reports and the session transcript are intentionally kept out of the source commit.
