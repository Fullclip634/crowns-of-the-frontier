# Worker tools, resource supply and HUD progress

The installed Blender LTS pipeline authored a solid axe, pick, hammer and sickle for the five villager variants. `node scripts/blender/build-worker-tools.mjs` writes an editable `.blend` and staged GLBs under `.tools/art-candidate/worker-tools/`. The tool patch retains the existing bodies, clothing, rigs, other 121 catalogue roots, and shared tree artwork. The current Low/Near catalogues contain the new tools; Balanced was recomposed from the current sources with `node scripts/blender/build-balanced.mjs --directory <staged catalogue>`. Its 126 models, exact visible triangle positions, UVs and colors, and measured normal error pass the precision checks. The shared tree GLB remains byte identical. Low stays within its 4.125 MiB budget.

Woodcutting now uses a two-handed pose: the right hand retains the axe grip, the left reaches the shaft, and the cutting plane follows the forward stroke. The actual Low/Balanced/Near rigs were checked at 101 phases per cycle for grip contact, finite joints and smooth transitions. Villagers navigate to the closer wood range through the normal path and collision rules. An extra final path segment is accepted only when its segment is clear. Fractional grid offsets (128 open approaches), water, hostile walls, saved routes, AI assignment and network work observation have focused regressions. Mining, building and food tools are solid Blender geometry but still use their existing work poses; their own contact calibration remains to do.

Newly generated trees, berries, stone, gold and fish hold twice the previous natural supply, including custom battles. Harvest rates and explicitly placed resource amounts are unchanged. Save and restore retain the exact depleted amount. Gold deposits use a warm ore body and separate metallic inclusions; stone deposits keep their rock material. The top resource bar now shows distinct original SVG icons before each amount and visible FOOD, WOOD, GOLD, STONE and POP labels on desktop and phone layouts, with full accessible quantities.

Custom battles now include an Alliance resources panel. It shows food, wood, gold and stone for each teammate, keeps enemy treasuries hidden in multiplayer snapshots, and lets the player send a chosen integer amount. The server checks team membership, living players, resource type, available balance and the transfer limit before changing either treasury. The recipient and sender receive a gift notice; balances update from the authoritative match state. Solo AI allies use the same transfer rules.

Verification: the full Node regression suite and production build passed after recomposing Balanced. Built gameplay was inspected in WebGPU High at 1024×600, WebGPU Low at 568×320, and WebGL Medium at 740×360. All three displayed golden ore, separate stone, five visible resource labels and icons, and no horizontal overflow; screenshots and measurements are in `artifacts/resource-presentation-browser/`. These browser checks are functional evidence, not performance measurements on a physical phone. The broad RTS quality goal remains active; mining contact, match length/balance, device performance, and multiplayer match play need further work and external player/device validation.

## AI construction route safety

The 16 October Highlands/Vast reproduction showed an AI mine foundation covering a spearman and cutting off his move order. `commanderBuild` now evaluates its proposed solid buildings against nearby allied combat routes using a temporary navigation grid. It skips a site that covers a troop or makes a previously reachable order unreachable, and accepts the site when pathfinding finds a detour. The check leaves the live simulation revision, current orders and cached routes unchanged. A saved-state replay of the exact mine location confirms the old site is still geometrically buildable, the new guard rejects it, and the AI selects another valid site. Four focused regressions and the full 466-test suite passed; production build passed. This addresses this construction failure mode, while crowding, transport routing and performance still need broader match-scale evaluation.
