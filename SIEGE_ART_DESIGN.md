# Siege art and rendering

The battering ram and trebuchet use original solid Blender geometry. The ram has a timber chassis, individual thick canopy planks, braced posts, suspension chains and a bound timber with a forged striking face. The trebuchet has braced A frames, axle bearings, a continuous throwing beam through its pivot, a fixed counterweight box, sling ropes, a solid pouch and a windlass. Ownership uses solid colored plates. The four wheels have enclosing iron tyres, wooden felloes, spokes, hubs and axle pins.

English Heritage's [siege warfare overview](https://www.english-heritage.org.uk/learn/story-of-england/medieval/siege-warfare/) informed the distinction between the counterweight on the short arm and the projectile sling on the long arm. These are game-scale original designs, not an archaeological reconstruction or a physical simulation of a trebuchet. Existing unit costs, damage, range and combat timing remain unchanged.

`scripts/blender/author-siege.py` replaces only `unit:ram` and `unit:catapult` in a separate copy of the editable 126-model master. It verifies that every source part encloses a solid and that the other 124 model hierarchies and vertices are unchanged. The export combines pieces only within their material and animation joint; the editable master retains individual pieces. High adds restrained edge bevels. Performance and Balanced retain the same structural silhouette and mechanisms. Runtime palette batching keeps each engine in one opaque instanced draw with four wheel joints and one weapon joint in addition to its stationary frame.

The unit pose texture now updates changed rows after the first complete upload. Unchanged Float32 poses do not request another upload. Pending rows remain dirty until Three confirms an upload, so a skipped draw cannot lose an update. Context restoration requests a complete upload. Color rendering, shadow depth and picking read the same matrices. The implementation follows the installed Three r180 DataTexture uploader: a partial update range must fit one texture row. This reduces upload work; it does not establish a frame-rate guarantee or reduce model detail, shadow resolution, render scale or a manually selected preset.

The Low catalogue content ceiling is now 4.125 MiB, with the original triangle-ratio gate retained. The solid engines add about 70 KiB (1.7%) to the previous 4,165,632-byte catalogue after export grouping. This is an explicit download tradeoff for complete mechanisms on lower presets. An experimental whole-catalogue repack was rejected because it increased size and changed unrelated normals; the other 124 compressed models remain unchanged.

The project still needs physical phone/laptop measurements, player feedback and broader art, interface and game-balance work before a release-quality claim can be made. Software-browser screenshots and upload counts are useful regression evidence, not proof of AAA visual parity or universal performance.
