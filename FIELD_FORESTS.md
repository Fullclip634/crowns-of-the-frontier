# Field forests and occasional lone trees

Fresh maps place most field wood in irregular, connected groves, with occasional lone trees in the open country. Up to five percent of the existing field trees, capped at 48, are reserved for individual placements before the groves grow. Individual trees are spaced apart, and groves maintain clearance around them. Starting woodlines keep their existing layout.

Composition repositions the existing field trees after starting forests are planted. It does not add trees, refill resources, replace IDs, move gold/stone/food, consume the simulation random stream or change the tree meshes. Existing saves retain their exact original locations and depleted amounts; they are never regrouped on load. Start a new battle to use the new layout.

Placement keeps building and troop clearance, starting build space, starting entrances, dry land, bridge/harbor clearings and gaps between independent groves. Jittered positions are rechecked so a shoreline tree cannot shift into water. Small tails extend accepted groves rather than forming unintended extra single trees. A local spatial index bounds placement queries. Composition runs at map creation, with no new per-frame system, scene meshes or forest draw batches. Visibility can still change how many existing trees the renderer draws in a particular view; this is not a guarantee of identical frame times.

`tests/field-forests.test.js` checks resource identities/counts/amounts, non-wood placement, deterministic results without advancing the live RNG, sparse individual trees, open eight-kingdom routes across seven biomes and three custom sizes for two seeds, and exact saved-layout preservation. The fixture verifies five isolated trees out of 100 field trees with at least six world units of clearance. Existing starting-forest tests cover additional seeds/sizes, settlement supply, army exits, clear starting units, construction space, harvesting and multiplayer save behavior.

The previous all-grouped release was visually checked on desktop WebGPU and emulated phone WebGL. Its reference eight-kingdom Vast Highlands seed 512 retained 2,085 entities, 1,779 total trees and 1,067,400 wood. The sparse-tree revision retains the same resource budget; increased tree density and asset changes are separate decisions requiring their own performance checks.

The sparse revision produced painted Highlands and Winter captures on desktop WebGPU High (1366 by 768) and emulated phone WebGL Performance (568 by 320), with no page errors, fatal graphics failures or horizontal overflow. Highlands had 29 isolated trees out of 583 field trees; Winter had 35 out of 709. Highlands retained the exact entity count, total trees, wood supply and simulation RNG state above. Its desktop reference frame still used 23 draw calls. These paused rendering observations are not sustained frame-rate measurements.
